An open build, made with a kid

Two buttons.
No screen.

A small aluminium box for a child, and an iPhone app for the parent who lives somewhere else. Press Record, talk, press again, and the parent hears it. When an answer arrives, Play glows.

01 · What it is

Presence, not a phone call.

A call needs both people free at once, and a child on a video call often just goes quiet. A box that holds a voice until someone is ready to hear it fits how a child stays close to someone who isn't there.

For the child: the lights are the whole display

For the parent: an iPhone app

  • One conversation, both directions, the whole archive. Each message you send shows when it reached the box and when it was played.
  • The box screen shows signal, last check-in, quiet hours and volume, and explains any fault in words. The child never has to.
  • A push when the child sends something, and when the box hasn't checked in for hours.
No red light, no micThe mic is powered from the pin that lights Record. That's wiring, not software.
Nothing is ever lostAudio is on disk while the child talks, and is only deleted after the server confirms a checked copy.
Private by designEnd-to-end encrypted. No transcription, no analytics.
Built for a school bagIts own LTE, so no Wi-Fi to set up. Short bumps are ignored; a travel lock stops the rest.

03 · Drawn to scale

Every hole, every wire.

Drawn from the build photos and hardware/cad/layout.json. The box is a 1590DD-size die-cast clone, 188 × 119 × 37.5 mm. Clones differ by a millimetre or two, so measure yours before drilling.

Two 16 mm button holes (step drill to 15, file to 16), a 2 mm mic hole and a 19-hole grille over the speaker.

Power and radio come in through the top edge: a USB-C panel socket (22.3 mm hole) and the antenna's SMA bulkhead (6.5 mm).

The Pi sits on the LTE HAT on nylon standoffs; foam presses the speaker to the grille. Wires are soldered, because plugs shake loose in a bag.

The wire that matters most: the mic is powered from the Record button's red LED tab, so no software can turn the mic on with the light off.

Physical pin numbers, pin 1 at the SD-card end; dot colours are the wire colours used. Must match box/dadbox/hw/pi.py. 3.3 V logic, ≤16 mA per pin.

04 · How it works

Box, server, phone.

Three pieces that follow one written contract, docs/PROTOCOL.md. Only the box and the phone hold the key.

Recording16 kHz audio goes to disk as the child talks. On stop: Opus at 16 kbps, encrypted, checked and saved. Only then the green pulse.
SendingResumable uploads in 32 KB pieces. Without a signal it waits, and the child never hears about a network.
ReceivingPlugged in, the box checks in every minute. Oldest message first, one per press of Play.

08 · Lessons

What went wrong, so you can skip it.

Power

USB-C couplers work one way up

A socket-to-socket coupler passes only one CC wire, so the plug worked one way round. Use a socket with a cable tail.

Audio

A wire on GAIN killed an amp

Its ground landed on the neighbouring pin; afterwards it scratched even on silence. Check grounds before power.

Linux

overlayroot puts /data in RAM

The stock read-only overlay covers every mount. Without recurse=0, the “safe” partition lives in memory.

Modem

It isn't who it says it is

The SIM7670G shows up as Qualcomm 05c6:9330, and at DIALMODE=1 forwards nothing. Use 0.

Protocol

Re-flashing reused a number

A fresh card restarted message numbering and the upload stuck. The server now tells the box where to continue.

Boot

Hold the power key low

GPIO 4 floats high at boot, and a long press turns the modem off. gpio=4=op,dl holds it low.
