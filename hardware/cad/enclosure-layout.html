How the planning values were reached: outside 188 × 120 × 37.5 from the listing. Walls are about 3 mm at the rim, plus about 1 mm of casting draft toward the tub floor, which gives 180 × 112. The tub floor and the lid are about 3 mm each, which gives 31 mm of depth. Measure yours with calipers at the rim and at the floor, and use the smaller figures.

Your step drill steps in 2 mm: 5, 7, 9 … 23. So the 16 mm button holes are drilled at 15 and opened with a half-round file until the thread just goes through. At 17 the flange still covers the hole, but the button can wobble before the nut is tight. Drill the templates template-top-face.svg, template-back-wall.svg and template-tub-floor.svg. How each part is held

The six lid screws are the only heads on show. Everything under the lid is held by its own nut, by tape, or pressed up from below by foam on the tub floor. What sits on the tub floor is screwed or taped to it.

Wiring

The leads are soldered straight into the Pi's GPIO holes, with no header and no dupont plugs. A dupont plug stands about 22 mm above the Pi and would hit the lid. Soldered leads also stay put in a school bag.

Before you drill, and in what order

Measure these first

  • Inside length and width at the rim and at the tub floor. Enter the smaller of each above.
  • Depth from the rim to the tub floor, and the lid's thickness.
  • Where the screw bosses are: four corners only, or two more in the middle of the long walls. Measure their diameter.
  • The Pi-on-HAT stack from the bottom of the HAT to the top of the Pi. The plan assumes 23 mm with soldered leads.
  • Where the HAT's LTE antenna connector is, and whether its antenna cable already ends in an SMA bulkhead. If it does, you don't need the Delock pigtail.
  • Where the mic's sound hole is: on the chip's side or through the board.

Build order

  1. Update the three dimensions above and in layout.py. Print the templates.
  2. Mock-up in cardboard. Lay the parts in the tub and hold the speaker to a cardboard grille, and listen.
  3. Drill the lid, then the back wall, then the tub floor. Deburr every hole and blow out the chips.
  4. Fit the buttons, the antenna bulkhead and the power socket. Threadlock the nuts.
  5. Tape the speaker and the mic under their holes.
  6. Screw the stack to the tub floor on standoffs and tape down the amp. Glue on the foam block.
  7. Solder the harness and heat-shrink every joint. Tie the bundles to adhesive mounts.
  8. Bench test with the box open. Then close it and check the speaker is pressed up and nothing rattles.
